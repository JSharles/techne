# Techne keeps no schedule: the learner organises their own time

Techne planned the learner's week: a morning program, an afternoon program, six full days and a light seventh, a `SCHEDULE.md` it proposed and followed, and deviations it recorded as drift. When the learner wrote a new program, the plan had no room for it, and the learner was told, in effect, that they lacked the time. They asked for the opposite: to enrol in a course and start it at once, as on a course platform, and to manage their own time.

Techne therefore keeps no schedule. Enrolling in a program opens it at once. The learner opens any program they follow whenever they want, for as long as they want; when they come back without saying which, Techne resumes the one they were last working on, and asks only when nothing is open. A program advances in sessions of work on it — the store still counts closed blocks per program — and assessments come after every six sessions of a program rather than on a light day. A light session of reviews only is proposed, never imposed, when results degrade.

The method is untouched: lessons before practice, spaced reviews on calendar days, evidence and help levels all stay as they were. Only the timetable around them is gone.

## Consequences

- The `schedule` command, `SCHEDULE.md` and the drift record are removed; an existing `SCHEDULE.md` is ignored.
- Programs describe their balance of work (for example, two isolated sessions for each project session) instead of fixed mornings and afternoons; their `cadence` header is ignored.
- The first start asks which program to begin with, instead of enrolling the learner in every shipped program.
